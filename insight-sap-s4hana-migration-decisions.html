Planning an SAP S/4HANA migration: seven decisions to make early

TechSphire Insights · 16 September 2026 · 7 min read

With mainstream maintenance for SAP ECC ending in 2027, migration planning can't wait. These are the decisions that shape cost, risk and timeline more than any others.

SAP has committed to mainstream maintenance for SAP ECC 6.0 until the end of 2027, with optional extended maintenance available at additional cost until 2030. For most organisations that makes an S/4HANA programme a question of when and how, not whether. The programmes that run smoothly make a handful of big decisions early and deliberately.

1. Your transition approach

A system conversion (often called brownfield) converts your existing system, keeping history and custom code. A new implementation (greenfield) starts clean on standard processes. A selective data transition combines elements of both. The right choice depends on how well your current processes serve the business, how much custom code you carry and how much history you need online.

2. Deployment model

Decide between public cloud, private cloud and on-premises deployment early, because it shapes the degree of customisation possible, your upgrade cadence and your operating model. Many organisations now evaluate this alongside SAP's subscription offerings, which bundle software, infrastructure and services.

3. How much to standardise

A migration is the best opportunity in a decade to retire customisations that no longer differentiate the business. Establish a "clean core" principle — keep extensions outside the core system where possible — and give process owners the authority to adopt standard processes.

4. Custom code

Run a custom code analysis early. It is common to find that a large share of custom objects are no longer used. Retiring them reduces testing effort and risk; the remainder needs remediation or redesign as side-by-side extensions.

5. Data

Data quality issues are among the most frequent causes of delay. Decide how much history to migrate, who owns each data domain and how cleansing will happen before migration — not during cutover. Archiving old data can shorten conversion downtime considerably.

6. Integration and the wider landscape

Map every interface to and from your current ERP. The migration is a chance to modernise point-to-point integrations onto an integration platform and to decide which satellite systems should be retired, upgraded or replaced.

7. Programme governance and change

Successful programmes treat S/4HANA as a business change, not an IT upgrade. That means executive sponsorship, empowered process owners, a realistic testing strategy with automation, and a training and adoption plan that starts months before go-live.

Next steps

A structured readiness assessment — covering system analysis, custom code, data, integrations and business priorities — gives you a fact-based recommendation on approach, a high-level roadmap and a budget range. It is the single most effective way to de-risk the programme before major spend begins.
